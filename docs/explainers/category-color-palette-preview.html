The palette is not the category list. Thirteen categories exist because that is what onboarding offers; seventeen colours exist because a user creating a category of their own picks from this list, and a palette with no colour left in it is a palette that forces a duplicate on the first custom category.

Tile is the transactions row (36px, with its glyph). Dot is the same row's category cell (8px). Chip is the onboarding selector (12px). Slice is the dashboard donut's SVG fill. The glyph is the same on every row on purpose: the tile's job here is to show whether the paired -content colour keeps a mark legible, not to identify a category.

Why a third of the palette clears 3:1 in both themes, and the rest cannot

The Light and Dark columns are the WCAG contrast ratio of the colour against the bg-base-100 card it is drawn on. The Expensa themes (PET-74) author every colour as an exact hex, so each figure is computed from those values directly and cross-checked in headless Chromium rather than estimated. 3:1 is the non-text bar, which is why a figure below it is badged as failing.

Six of the seventeen clear 3:1 in both themes - primary, secondary, info, success, error and base-content/50 - where the stock themes managed two. The other eleven are legible in exactly one, and that is a property of the pairing rather than of any assignment here: each colour's -content sits at the opposite end of the lightness range, which is exactly what keeps a glyph legible on its own tile (every pairing measures 3.4:1 or better, both themes) and exactly what makes the same colour near-invisible as a fill on the page's own surface in one theme. Re-picking cannot fix it, because seventeen categories cannot all be distinct and all clear 3:1 in both.

base-content/50 is the seventeenth entry and is not a semantic pair at all; it is the page's own ink at half strength, and it clears 3:1 in both themes. That is why the Uncategorized fallback carries it: the backend folds orphaned spend onto that row, so it can hold the largest donut slice and must not be invisible.

PET-64 accepted the rest for the twelve category templates on a stated argument: the donut ring is aria-hidden, its legend names every slice in real text, and every glyph clears 3:1 on its own tile, so colour carries no information WCAG 1.4.11 governs.

Two pairs are close on purpose, and a third was

Measured in OKLab against the Expensa themes, where roughly 0.10 is the floor for telling two categories apart:

  • ΔE 0.020   Education / Travel   primary-content / secondary-content
  • ΔE 0.060   Personal care / Gifts   accent-content / success-content
  • ΔE 0.137   Groceries / Utilities   success / accent - 0.060 under the stock themes; PET-74 separated it past the floor

Kept rather than re-picked. Breaking Education / Travel would force one onto a dark tile, since primary-content ("Lavender") and secondary-content ("Blush") are the two near-white tiles and a dark replacement would break the picker label it ships under. What makes it safe is that every category carries its own icon, which is the channel that separates them, and it is why the icon map shipped in the same ticket as the palette rather than after it.

The icons have spares now too

This section used to say the opposite, and the correction is the point. PET-64 shipped thirteen icons, which is exactly what its seeded categories carry and not one more, so every offered glyph was already spoken for and the first category a user invented had to reuse one. The colours never had that problem: seventeen back thirteen categories, so four were always spare.

That asymmetry read as deliberate and was not. PET-65 took the icons to sixty-four, which is the same slack applied to the other channel. The colour side of this page is unchanged by it.

Where each column comes from

  • Token, order, Label, Picker - COLOUR_SEED in backend/src/database/central/template-seed.ts. The order is the picker's order, and the label is the admin's to rename, which is the whole reason it is a column instead of a function of the token.
  • The allowlist itself - COLOUR_TOKENS in backend/src/database/central/template-tokens.ts. A category stores the token verbatim, never a hex: primary is valued differently per theme, so a stored hex would record one value and paint the other half the time.
  • Light and Dark - COLOUR_CONTRAST, beside the allowlist in the same file.
  • Used by - CATEGORY_SEED in template-seed.ts for the twelve, and FALLBACK_CATEGORY in backend/src/database/user/starter-categories.ts for Uncategorized, which is deliberately not a template.
  • What the picker actually receives - GET /api/templates/palette, served by backend/src/templates/templates.service.ts and shaped by backend/src/templates/dto/palette-response.dto.ts. It returns enabled rows only, so it answers with sixteen.
  • The four class strings - CATEGORY_TILE, CATEGORY_DOT, CATEGORY_FILL and CATEGORY_ICON in frontend/src/components/ui/categoryColour.ts, each keyed by the contract's own union so an eighteenth token breaks that build until every map covers it.
  • The table row storing it - colour_templates in backend/src/database/central/schema.ts: token, label, sort order, enabled, tombstone. It lives in the central database because it belongs to nobody and a super admin edits it.

The sibling file category-colors-icons-description-preview.html is the categories' own sign-off artifact and draws the thirteen rows a user actually gets.

Validation checks the allowlist and never the enabled flag, which is the split to keep in mind reading the Picker column: a category already carrying a since-disabled colour still saves and still renders. That is why error-content ships disabled rather than absent, and why the picker's sixteen is legitimately a strict subset of the API's seventeen. circle-question-mark is the glyph on every tile above rather than circle-help, which is a deprecated alias in the installed lucide 1.29.0.
